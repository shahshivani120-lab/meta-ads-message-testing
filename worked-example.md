# Worked example: the real programme, step by step

This is the real 2026 Meta Ads programme for RestoGenius, an Australian restaurant point-of-sale (POS) SaaS selling to independent cafés and restaurants. Every number comes from Meta boosted-post results and Ads Manager (screenshots captured 24 Nov 2026). Full evidence is in the [case study PDF](https://shahshivani120-lab.github.io/case-studies/Meta_Ads_Case_Study_Shivani_Shah.pdf).

---

## Step 1 · The problem

Busy venue owners rarely fill in forms. But they will reply to a message. So the goal was **conversations with owners**, and the question was: *which message makes them reply?*

## Step 2 · The set-up

| Setting | Choice |
|---|---|
| Objective | Messaging ("Get more messages" on boosted posts) |
| Destinations | WhatsApp and Messenger |
| Budget | ₹280–₹500 a day per ad |
| Rule | One hook per ad |
| Period | Feb–Aug 2026: 17 boosted posts; then Ads Manager campaigns in Q4 |

## Step 3 · The themes tested

| Theme | Example hook (from the real ads) |
|---|---|
| Free hardware / no upfront cost | "Still paying thousands for POS systems? There's a better way." |
| Named price ($89) | "Still paying separately for your POS, online ordering and Uber Eats?" |
| Transaction fees | "Still on 2%? Your POS provider is making more from your restaurant…" |
| Hidden add-on costs | "Paying extra for Uber Eats sync? Xero? EFTPOS?" |
| Menu insights | (menu performance angle) |
| Operational pain | "Running a café already takes long hours…" |
| Delivery commissions | "How much of every delivery order are you giving away in commission?" |

## Step 4 · Results by theme

17 boosted posts, ₹73.4K spend, 57 conversations (average ₹1,288 per conversation).

| Theme | Ads | Conversations | Cost per conversation | Spend (calculated) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Free hardware / no upfront cost | 4 | 17 | ₹686 | ₹11,662 |
| $89 all-in-one price, named | 3 | 17 | ₹958 | ₹16,286 |
| Transaction fees ("Still on 2%?") | 1 | 9 | ₹1,309 | ₹11,781 |
| Hidden add-on costs, no price | 3 | 7 | ₹2,136 | ₹14,952 |
| Menu insights | 1 | 2 | ₹2,241 | ₹4,482 |
| Operational pain | 3 | 2 | ₹2,611 | ₹5,222 |
| Delivery commissions | 2 | 3 | ₹3,021 | ₹9,063 |
| **Total** | **17** | **57** | **₹1,288** | **₹73,448** |

*Spend per theme = conversations × cost per conversation, rounded. These are the example rows in the workbook.*

## Step 5 · What the data showed

1. **Free hardware won:** 17 chats at ₹686, **~4x cheaper** than delivery-commission hooks (₹3,021).
2. **Naming the price halved the cost:** "$89 all-in-one" ran at ₹958; the same idea without a price cost ₹2,136.
3. **Fees got attention:** "Still on 2%?" was the most-seen ad (5,159 views) and started 9 chats in one run.
4. **Pain alone didn't work:** "long hours" and "wrong orders" started 2 chats from 3 ads. Owners recognise the problem, but they reply to an offer.

## Step 6 · Creative fatigue

Cost per conversation by period:

| Period | Cost per chat | Chats | What was running |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Feb–Mar | ₹1,461 | 5 | — |
| Apr | ₹921 | 18 | Free hardware + "Still on 2%?" |
| May–Jun | ₹1,252 | 20 | — |
| Jul–Aug | ₹1,751 | 14 | Delivery-commission tests |

Re-runs of the same creative:

| Ad | First run | Re-run | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| "Still paying thousands for POS?" (8 days later) | ₹512 | ₹551 | +8% |
| "$89 all-in-one" (early May → Aug 4) | ₹517 | ₹1,347 | +160% |

**Decision:** stop re-boosting old winners. Every new push gets a fresh visual or hook on a proven offer, and runs in Ads Manager where frequency can be watched.

## Step 7 · Applying it in Q4

| Campaign (Ads Manager, US$) | Status | Results | Cost per result | Spent |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Black Friday: free hardware, 22 Nov–7 Dec | Active | 6 chats | US$20.67 | US$124.00 |
| WhatsApp chats, Brisbane, video ad | Ended 24 Nov | 4 chats | US$42.55 | US$170.19 |
| Halloween offer | Ended 8 Nov | 0 chats | — | US$64.92 |
| RG-Agent free trial, AU venue owners | Off | 25 page views | US$1.03 | US$25.81 |

- The winning offer led Black Friday at **US$20.67 per chat**, about half the Brisbane video campaign.
- The seasonal Halloween offer started no chats, so it ended and the budget went to the proven offer.

## Step 8 · What I'd do next (and what you should copy)

- Track which WhatsApp conversations become demos and customers, so reviews report **cost per customer**, not cost per message.
- Refresh creative every 2–3 weeks and A/B test hooks inside Ads Manager rather than re-boosting.
- Keep the winning offers (free hardware, the $89 price) as the core; retire delivery-commission and pain-only hooks.
- Run everything from one ad account and currency so spend can be compared end to end.

## Account totals for context

79 WhatsApp/Messenger contacts in 2026 (1 Jan–10 Nov) from ~US$1,050 (₹93.2K), an average of ₹1,371 across 19 messaging campaigns (~US$13 per contact). One boost set up by a colleague (3 Jun) is excluded from the theme analysis.
