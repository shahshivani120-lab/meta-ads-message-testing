# Creative fatigue and scaling

Winning ads wear out, especially when the audience is small (for example, business owners in one city). The same people see the ad again and again, stop noticing it, and costs rise.

## How to spot fatigue

| Signal | Where | Warning level |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency rising | Ads Manager → Frequency | Above ~3 in a week for a small audience |
| CTR falling | CTR (all), week over week | Down 30%+ from the ad's first week |
| Cost per conversation rising | Results log | Up 50%+ from the first run |
| Same people commenting | Ad comments | Repeat commenters, "seen this already" |

**Re-run change %** (the workbook calculates it):

```
Re-run change = (re-run cost per conversation − first-run cost) ÷ first-run cost
```

From the case study:

| Ad | First run | Re-run | Change | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "Still paying thousands for POS?" (re-run 8 days later) | ₹512 | ₹551 | **+8%** | Still fresh. Keep running |
| "$89 all-in-one" (early May → Aug 4) | ₹517 | ₹1,347 | **+160%** | Fatigued. The offer still worked elsewhere, but this creative was worn out |

**Rule of thumb:** under +25% = fine; +25–50% = watch; over +50% = refresh.

## How to refresh (keep the offer, change the wrapper)

When a winning theme fatigues, don't drop the offer. Change one of these:

| Refresh | Example (free hardware theme) |
|---|---|
| New first line, same offer | "Still paying thousands for POS?" → "New POS hardware. $0 upfront." |
| New visual | Product photo → owner photo → short video |
| New format | Single image → carousel → Reel |
| New angle on the same offer | "Free hardware" → "Switch for $0" → "Keep the cash for your next hire" |
| New proof | Add a real customer quote or number |

Plan **3–4 creative variations per winning offer** so you can rotate them. In small audiences, aim to refresh every **2–3 weeks**.

## Scaling a winner

Once a theme has won across 2+ ads:

1. **Move it to Ads Manager** with a clear campaign structure and end date (see [campaign structure](campaign-structure.md)).
2. **Raise budget gradually.** Increase by roughly 20–30% every few days rather than doubling overnight; large jumps can push costs up while delivery re-adjusts.
3. **Widen the audience before you raise budget a lot.** More people = slower fatigue.
4. **Add a deadline** if there's a real one (season, event, limited setups). The case study's Black Friday campaign used the winning free-hardware offer with a date and got chats at **US$20.67**, about half the cost of a general WhatsApp video campaign (US$42.55).
5. **Keep a small test budget running** (10–20% of spend) for new hooks, so you always have the next winner ready.
6. **Kill what doesn't work fast.** A seasonal Halloween offer in the case study spent US$64.92 with zero chats, so it ended and the budget went to the proven offer.

## Scaling checklist

- [ ] Winning theme confirmed on 2+ ads with 5+ conversations each
- [ ] 3–4 creative variations ready
- [ ] Reply capacity ready for more chats (people and scripts)
- [ ] Qualified rate checked (not just cheap chats)
- [ ] End date and budget cap set
- [ ] Weekly frequency and cost check in the calendar
